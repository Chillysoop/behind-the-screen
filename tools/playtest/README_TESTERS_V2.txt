BEHIND THE SCREEN v2 - The Museum of Nightmares (playtest build)
=================================================================

Thank you for playing! You're the Dungeon Master for four friends who meet once a
week to play a fantasy tabletop game. This build runs one real published adventure
as a prologue: The Museum of Nightmares by Luke Russell (from the DMs Guild),
adapted for private testing. A gnome curator has a problem with his museum after
dark, and something upstairs is collecting statues.

You don't need to know D&D, and there's no way to "lose": every ending is a real
ending, and the prologue sets up the full campaign that comes next.


HOW IT GOES
-----------
- Session Zero: meet the table, then pick why each hero came to the museum. Each
  player leans toward one of two reasons, and each reason pays off in one room.
- Each week, plan the next session on the museum map: which rooms to prepare and
  how to run each one. An unprepared room still plays, just worse.
- Game night: the party walks the museum room by room, each room on its own board
  drawn from the book's maps. The floors are open: the players pick where to go,
  and they'll go back to a room only with a reason (a corner they missed, a hint
  from you, the shop, a rest, or running away). When a card slides across your
  screen, you make the call. A harsh call lands better when the party took
  chances knowingly (the "fair warning" count on the planning board).
- Euryale's card: every room the party clears, skips or botches leaves a mark on
  the boss's card. The last session is built from what they did.
- What they're thinking: now and then a pale cloud appears over a player's panel.
  Something's on their mind. Click it and roll to read them (Insight): the better
  you know that friend and the better you read your table (Empathy), the easier it
  is. A good read hears the thought; a near miss gets the gist; a miss means you
  ask them straight out or let it be. Then answer it, or let it be.
- Maps: "All maps" on the planning board (and "Maps" on game night) opens every map
  you can put on the table. Give a room a different map, open a side room off any
  room for the party to wander into, change the next scene's map mid-game, or make
  your own in the map maker, with the museum's pieces in its Museum tab.
- Between sessions: the players ask for things, loose ends from earlier rooms come
  back, and the museum's people help or hinder the party for how they were treated.

The prologue usually takes three to five sessions. Play it in one go, or stop after
any session: the game saves when each session ends, and "Continue" on the title card
picks up with the next one. (If you quit in the middle of a session, that session
starts over.)


STARTING
--------
1. Unzip the whole folder somewhere (your Desktop is fine). Don't run it from inside the zip.
2. Open "BehindtheScreen.exe".
3. Windows may say "Windows protected your PC", because this test build isn't signed.
   Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
4. A short note says what this prototype is (and isn't). Click "Agree" to start.

It needs 64-bit Windows 10 or 11. If the window is bigger than your screen, double-click
its title bar to maximize it: the game scales to fit any size.


CONTROLS
--------
Almost everything is the mouse. A few keys help:
  Enter            the next step (run game night, see the report, next session)
  N                your notes: the museum, who's who, the party
  Esc              close your notes, a sheet, a conversation or a pop-up
  M                music on / off
On game night:
  Space            skip ahead
  F / G            faster / slower
  Tab              the night's log
  H                your own rolls: behind your screen or in the open
  T                a gentle timer on each call
  C                a photo of the table
  1 to 6           pick a card's option (or an answer to a thought you opened)
  Right mouse drag look around    Mouse wheel  zoom    R  reset the camera


WHAT'S NOT FINAL
----------------
The museum's monsters borrow minis from the earlier build, so a scarab swarm may
look like something else for now. (The rooms have their own boards now.) The rules, the
story and the choices are what's being tested.


WHAT WE'D LIKE FROM YOU
-----------------------
- Play it the way you'd naturally play. There are no wrong choices; we want to
  see what you do, not whether you're good at it.
- After each session the game asks two quick questions. A click each.
- After the last session there's a recap of your table's journey and a short
  survey, with two optional typed answers. Honest is more useful than nice.
- Then send us your playtest log (see below). That's it!


SENDING YOUR LOG
----------------
Double-click "Open my playtest log folder.bat" in this folder. (Or press Windows+R,
type  %APPDATA%\behind_the_screen  and press Enter.)

Send us the file  playtest_log.jsonl  from that folder. If the game ever showed a
"Something broke" box, please send  error_log.txt  too.

What's in the log: the choices you made in the game, the game's own numbers for each
session, how long prep and game night took, and your survey answers. Nothing about
you or your computer. You can open it in Notepad to see for yourself.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
- "Something broke" on screen: the game wrote what happened to error_log.txt (see
  above). Close the game and start it again; "Continue" takes you back to the start
  of the session you were on (or the next one, if the session had already ended).
- It won't start at all: tell us your Windows version and what you saw.
