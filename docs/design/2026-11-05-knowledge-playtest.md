# Knowledge playtest, November 5 (third build)

What the players know against what the DM knows, compared to *The Museum of Nightmares* (page numbers in the reader's
own PDF; every word about the book here is a paraphrase).

**How it was run.** Nine whole prologues from the headless sim (`tools/sim/museum.lua`, seeds 101 to 103, each with a
careful, a random and a careless DM), about 8,500 lines. Three reviewers who hadn't seen the code read every line
against the book's pages, one per part of the museum (the street to the Chamber; the Hall of Paintings to the
offices; the World of Tomorrow to the thanks, plus the chat, recaps and breaks). Each finding below was checked
against the logs. The rule they read by: the friends know only what their heroes have seen, heard or been told at
the table in this playthrough; the DM knows the book.

## The short version

Most of the book's room secrets hold until play reveals them. The hedges stay hedges, the statue stays still, the
knight wakes only for a closer look, the construct's and the Lecturer's backstories stay the DM's, Telpur's nature
stays a mystery, and the Witness stays subtle. But knowledge reaches the table in seven repeated ways, and the
three worst happen in nearly every run:

- **The friends propose plans only the DM's card knows.** "Let's work out the ritual" in the Chamber, in 9 of 9 runs,
  when nobody had said there is a ritual. "Let's bargain: her sisters for her leaving" when nothing had said her
  sisters are trapped.
- **Euryale is named before anyone has told the table her name.** It happens in the "Last time" recap ("climbed the
  stairs to Euryale's door") and in the lair's first line, in 8 of 9 runs. In the three careless runs, nobody had told
  them there was a Gorgon at all.
- **The Hunting Grounds announce the hidden pack, and where it hides, as the party walks in.** "Something in the
  exhibits is hunting", then "Something shifts in the jungle diorama. Something in the cave model answers it." This
  happens in 9 of 9 runs, and then the narration names them as kruthik huntlings. The book keeps the name for its
  guide.

**One decision is yours: is the Gorgon the premise or a secret?** In the book, the players learn it only from
Flitwidget's confession, which needs Persuasion DC 12 or Intimidation DC 16, or later from Telpur (p6, p16). In 5 of
these 9 runs nobody pried it out of him. In the game, Session Zero tells the table "a Gorgon has got loose in it",
and Session Zero's own lines talk about the Gorgon. That's why "What do you make of the Gorgon?" in the gift shop
reads as foreknowledge.
- (a) **Keep it as the premise.** The players know, as they would from the title and the cover. The heroes learn it
  in the street. Then in-character lines about the Gorgon wait for that reveal.
- (b) **Keep it secret, like the book.** The premise says only that something in the museum has got loose. Session
  Zero's lines lose the Gorgon. Flitwidget, Telpur and the gorgon book become the ways in.

Either way: **the confession itself should name Euryale**, as the book's does. Today the pry's success line says "A
Gorgon in a jar, a jar that broke" without her name, so even a table that earned the secret never hears it.

## 1. The friends propose what only the DM's card knows

A friend's vote line is written from the card's option (`want`), so it can say what the card says, and the card is
the DM's.

- **The ritual, 9 of 9 runs.** "Let's work out the ritual while someone holds the guardian off" and "Let's do the
  ritual". The table had only heard of jars, a cracked tablet and very old glyphs. The word "ritual" is on the DM's
  goal card ("by mace or by ritual"). Book p11 to p13: that a water ritual can still the guardian is the DM's, found
  by reading the tablet with the notebook. Related: "Three skulls. I hate it. Somebody find the off switch." (4 runs).
- **"Her sisters for her leaving", 2 runs (102 careless, 103 careless).** The table had seen a tapestry of three
  snake-eyed sisters. Nothing said they were trapped or that she wants them. In 103 careless the table chose it and
  it worked. Book p25 and p27: the sealed pots and her wish to free them are the DM's.
- **"Let's save it for the Gorgon", in the World of Tomorrow (102 careless).** In this run nobody had said Gorgon (but
  see the decision above).
- **The falcon jar (3 runs).** The card has a hero carry the falcon jar to the pedestal "anyway", and then "Pip squints
  at the last line again, gets the gist" is said aloud. The pedestal and the falcon jug had never been described.
  Book p13: each tablet line is its own History check.

## 2. Stock friend lines that assume a path this run didn't take

Lines written for the usual run, said in runs where their premise never happened.

- "If Flitwidget's right about upstairs" and "whatever's upstairs" (4 runs, 8 lines). Flitwidget never says
  upstairs. In the book he says he doesn't know what they'll meet (p6); Telpur is the first to hint at upstairs
  (p16).
- "What do you make of the Gorgon?" asked of Telpur (101 careful, 101 random), with no Gorgon said in either run.
- "That bird under the dome. Six wings. A mythril singer?" and Thessaly's "It's a mythril singer" (5 of 6 office
  runs). The guess is always right and comes before any check. The wings were never described. Book p21: the
  name comes from the notes on the walls.
- "His desk. Letters, a map of the sea, a list of names." (5 of 6). Nobody had described the desk, and the sea
  anticipates the notebook's sunken city (p22).
- "Nobody look at her eyes. Nobody drink anything." (7 of 9 lairs). It's always said before any drink is offered, and
  in 4 runs no drink is ever offered.
- "Nobody sit down. Whatever you do, do not sit down." (4 of 7 lecture runs). Sitting to listen is what the drone's
  charm does, in the stat block (p37). The corpses on the benches that might hint at it are never described.
- "Gorgons come in threes. Where are her sisters?" (102 careful, with no cue at all).
- "Stay together. Whatever's in here hunts in packs." That they're pack hunters is one of the guide's facts (p20).
- "Were you really a demon?" to Telpur (102 careless), before anyone had mentioned a demon.
- Things named before they were shown: "that painting of a stormy sea" (101 careful), "the cleaning cupboard" and
  "the dwarf king's beard" (102 careless), "the blankets over the window" and "the blackboard" in the lecture
  theatre, "Bigger than the jackal jug" one line before the jackal jug.
- "Don't put your fingers in its mouth" just before the crocodile's jaw snaps (103 careful), "Don't read one. I've
  got a feeling" just before the notes turn out to put readers to sleep.

## 3. Narration names or gives away hidden things

- **The Hunting Grounds** (9 of 9). Two problems: the hidden pack and two of its hiding places are given on
  entering, and "you keep it in the shadows" is addressed to the DM. Book p19 and p20: the floor is messier and
  they feel watched. Five hide: two in the jungle, two in the cave model kept back as a surprise, and one posing as
  a statue.
- **Monster names from the stat blocks, said as they appear.** "kruthik huntlings" (9 of 9; the book keeps the name
  for the guide, p20), "the Knight of Shades" (the book's label for the DM, p15), "the briar boar" named while it's
  still a bush, as half the garden burns (2 fire runs, p10).
- **Euryale by name, on sight** (9 of 9): "Euryale waits among her statues in a white hood". Book p25: a tall woman
  in a white hood and purple cloak, at ease on a couch; nothing names her.
- **"A metal guard between them"** (9 of 9). In the book it's a statue of an armoured man (p11); that it guards
  anything is the DM's.
- **The plaque: "If they get lively, try the water"** (9 of 9). The book's plaque warns about the bushes and says
  water helps in an emergency (p10); "lively" tells them the hedges move.
- **The crocodile: "Pull its jaw back and water runs out"** (6 runs). In the book they find the tap by trying the jaw
  (p13).
- **The tracks: "Almost as if someone wanted it followed"** (5 runs). That the marks are bait is the DM's (p20).
- **The knight's tell on entering: "Its oils seem to shift"** (8 of 8). In the book the shifting comes when someone
  looks closely, just before it climbs out (p15); on entering it's a grim, perfect portrait.
- **The Lecturer's notes: "Whoever reads one is asleep before the end of the line"** (7 runs), said as the party
  picks them up.
- **The red switch: "HYPER DESTROYER MODE. Only if absolutely necessary."** (6 runs). The warning is the manual's,
  bought in the gift shop (p23, p29).

## 4. The DM's cards describe what the heroes see, and nobody says it

A card that's the DM's alone (not a party choice) describes the scene in its text. When the DM picks an option, the
option's line is said, and the description never is. Then the friends react to something they were never told.

- **The archivist's room** (6 of 6). The dark room, the body at the desk, and the walls papered with eyes are only
  on the card. Then "I'm going to need a minute after that body" and "This writing makes my head hurt" are said
  aloud. Book p21: this is the read-aloud.
- **The pond's tentacle** (9 of 9). "A grey tentacle slides up ... Everyone saw it" is on the card. Then the table
  hears "They'd been warned".
- **The five people with dice** (4 runs). The painting is in the book's read-aloud on entering (p14). Here it's only
  on the card, and "the painting of us. i'm not ok" turns up in the chat.
- **Flitwidget's Francs** (9 of 9). The green note with his face, and "Spend them in the gift shop!", are on the
  card. The table hears only the rule. With "Keep it a surprise" picked, they hear nothing at all. Book p7 has him
  explain the Francs and show the note.
- **"Incidents with the exhibits"** (2 runs): the card reacts to a line Flitwidget never said aloud in those runs.
- **The Mech's chest panel** (6 of 6). The numbers are only on the card, and an Arcana roll replaces the decoding
  (see section 7).

## 5. Recaps, the chat and between nights

- **"Climbed the stairs to Euryale's door"** in the recap (6 runs where her name had never been said; in 2 of them,
  nobody had said there was a Gorgon). Book p6 and p18: her name comes from Flitwidget's confession, and that she's at the top of the stair is
  the DM's.
- **The bracelet in the week 2 recap: "Against her eyes. Wear it upstairs."** (102 careless, 102 random). In 102
  careless it's the first "her" the table has heard of. It also puts her upstairs, which Flitwidget doesn't know
  (p6).
- **The cupboard door:** "if Euryale lifts her hood, she meets her own eyes" (101 careful). That her reflection is a
  risk to her is in her stat block (p35). Also, the stairs: "Pip glimpses bronze eyes past the curtain and looks
  away just in time" (2 runs).
- **Advantage "against her stare" said while she's still hooded** (4 runs), from the gorgon book or the bracelet.
- **Euryale knows of their thefts from the Lecturer** (4 runs), including twice after he was destroyed, and once when
  they'd never met him. That tells the table he's hers.
- **"i made a little map of the museum. don't look at it dev, it has spoilers"** (2 runs where no map was ever handed
  over).
- **Before the first night:** "the one with the gnome on the roof? yes. obsessed" (3 runs) describes the street
  scene before anyone has played it.
- **"Whatever has been killing people in this town is right here."** Nobody had told them about deaths (102 careless).

## 7. What the DM is given, against the book

These are fidelity rather than leaks. Many are deliberate simplifications. Each changes what the DM can run.

- **The Chamber's ritual is one roll** (3 runs). One success says the whole solution aloud ("Five gallons in the
  falcon, three in the jackal ...") and the guardian kneels. Book p13: one History check (DC 12, with the
  notebook) per tablet line, then the players work out the pouring, while the construct fights. The pedestal and
  its button are never described (0 of 9), and the falcon jug in only 3. The tablet is read with Religion in some
  runs (the book: History).
- **The Mech's code is one Arcana roll** (6 of 6). There are no numbers to decode, and no 30 seconds. The Commander
  rule, the subroutines and the power-down when the Commander drops are never used (p23, p29, p38). In the game,
  flipping the red switch is a roll that burns the Mech out (5 runs). In the book, Hyper Destroyer Mode is a mode
  the Commander switches on, with a Constitution save each turn after.
- **The huntlings stand and fight**, with a leader who rises again. In the book they're five identical hit-and-run
  hunters that hide, dodge and are rarely all in view, with one posing as a statue (p19, p20, p37). The survivors
  join Euryale (6 runs); the book doesn't link them.
- **Euryale's greeting, her drink and her answers are cut** in 6 of 9 runs, and in none of the nine does she answer
  a question. Book p25 and p26: she welcomes them and offers a drink, answers their questions, and only then gives
  her ultimatum. She also has helpers in her lair (8 of 9: the knight, the scarabs, the huntlings). In the book she
  is alone with her statues (p25 to p27).
- **The gift shop sells 4 of the book's 18 things** (p17). Missing among them: the guide to primeval creatures (the
  book's way to name and understand the huntlings) and the ear plugs (its answer to the Lecturer). The scholar's
  book is "on gorgon-worshippers"; in the book it's about the followers of a goddess of poison, and it helps with
  gorgons among others.
- **The donation box's boon** (p12, a d10 table of hour-long boons) isn't on the DM's card. The table hears
  "something warm settles over Pip" and nothing comes of it (4 runs).
- **The Sentinel's calmer side** (it won't chase people who back off, and a donation calms it; p12, p38) is never
  offered. In the game the case has a lock with a DC; in the book it's armoured glass (p11).
- **Flitwidget hands them a map of the museum** (5 runs). In the book he warns he doesn't know what's in there now
  (p6). The map is also why the friends can name rooms ahead.
- **Telpur knows what's upstairs** ("Clicking, mostly. And somebody who never, ever stops talking") and warns them
  about Euryale's soft talk (2 runs each). In the book he's never been upstairs, since stairs and a boot don't mix
  (p16).
- **An avoided knight waits in her lair** (5 runs). In the book, skipping it has no cost (p15).
- **The bird in the study is alive, and goes quiet near the notebook.** In the book it's stuffed under its dome, and
  its kind sing loudly near magic (p21).
- **Invented clues that point at the Gorgon:** "The Lady in the Jar" struck out on the leaflet, and a snake scratched
  on Flitwidget's plaque. They're earned by rolls, but the book doesn't have them. "A snake ... oh. Oh, right." (Sam,
  101 random) connects it to nothing said.
- **Numbers:** the construct has 45 HP (the book: 33), Euryale 104 (the book: 65; and three attacks while hooded,
  where the book has one), the Lecturer 31 (the book: 22). The lair huntlings have 18 (the book: 7). They look
  scaled for a party a level up; the game starts at level 2 and ends at 4, where the book goes from 1 to 3.

## Why today's checks passed

- The **book check** lets a hidden foe be named as its fight starts. The book keeps the name for longer (the guide
  for the huntlings, the DM's label for the knight). And the sheet doesn't hold the hunting floor's entry narration
  to "sees".
- The **knowledge check** looks for names, not for facts or plans ("the ritual", "her sisters", "upstairs", "don't
  drink"). It counts the premise as told, so the Gorgon is known from the start.
- Neither reads the DM's cards for a description nobody says aloud.

## Suggested fixes, in order

1. **A vote says only what the table knows.** Each option's `want` (and the friends' stock lines) gets a `knows`
   condition from the event log, or a version without the secret ("Let's see what the tablet says while someone
   holds it off"). The ritual, her sisters, the Gorgon, upstairs, the drink, sitting down, the mythril singer, the
   desk.
2. **Euryale's name arrives once, in play.** The confession names her. In runs where nobody pried, she introduces
   herself in her greeting. Every recap and narration line that names her waits for one of these.
3. **The Hunting Grounds enter as the book does.** The table hears that the floor is messier and they feel watched.
   The pack and its places come out when the huntlings strike or a check finds them. Their name comes from the
   guide or a check, not the narration. The same goes for the stat-block names (the Knight of Shades, the briar
   beasts).
4. **A card's description is said when it opens.** A DM card that describes what the heroes see gets a `see` line,
   narrated before the DM decides: the archivist's room, the tentacle, the dice painting, Flitwidget's Francs, the
   Mech's panel.
5. **The recaps, the chat and the between-night lines read from what was said,** not from the DM's cards (the
   bracelet, "spoilers", the gnome on the roof, the killings).
6. **The checks learn the rest.** The knowledge check gets the sheet's secret phrases and the cards' `want`s, and a
   scan for card descriptions never said. The book check holds names to the sheet's `named` (when the table may hear
   a foe's name, not just when it fights).
7. **Your call on the book's structure** (section 7): the ritual and the Mech as real puzzles, the huntlings' hit and
   run, Euryale's conversation, the shop's stock. These are bigger changes, worth deciding one by one.
