# Behind the Screen v2: The Museum of Nightmares

October 30, 2026. The full design, with the feedback table, the room-by-room plan and the open questions, is
the living doc "Behind the Screen v2: The Museum of Nightmares" (https://claude.ai/artifact/QXuhtYeFWy6rqiTXLimQbj).
This file is the short version that lives with the code.

## Why

The Discord feedback (Straps, Hotsoop, Tasia via Straps) says the table works and the story doesn't: random
hands of story cards made stories that didn't fit together, and nothing visibly remembered what the table did.
v2 keeps the four friends, Session Zero, the rules engine and game night, and replaces the story deck, factions,
seeds, the valley and the planner with a real published adventure, cut into cards that remember.

## The prologue

*The Museum of Nightmares* by Luke Russell (DMs Guild), as a one-shot prologue that takes as many sessions as the
table needs (three to five in testing). Strictly the book: nothing from the Silent Bells shows while it runs. The
main campaign after it will be the next adventure the user uploads, so nothing in the format is museum-specific.

## The loop

1. **Session Zero, then why each hero came.** Two of the book's background hooks per player, one they lean
   toward. Each hook pays off in one room.
2. **Prep on the museum map.** Rooms to ready within the week's 10 hours (an unprepped room plays worse), and the
   book's own advice as knobs (how to run a room; who would love or hate it). Euryale's card is on the board.
3. **Game night room by room.** The book's moments as calls, forks the table picks by what they want, and a cut
   upstairs to Euryale's thoughts when her card gains a mark.
4. **The report**, with what each room left behind.
5. **Between sessions:** a player request answered four ways (yes / with a laugh / no, with a reason / yes, but
   it's dangerous), loose ends that re-home a card the party skipped, favors with one help and one hurt.
6. **Boss night:** a showdown board (how she opens, how far she goes), a talk round, the fight, five endings,
   then the journey recap (route, marks, each hero's history in short lines, what each friend wants next).

## The ideas the feedback asked for

- **Living cards.** Thirteen marks on Euryale's card, each from something the party did, each changing who fights
  beside her, what the party brings, or how easily she talks (`game/data/museum/module.lua`, `M.marks`).
- **Harsh calls that feel earned.** Chances taken knowingly bank a fair warning; a harsh call spends one. Without
  one in the bank it costs trust and adds suspicion. A *Dark and dangerous* table takes the first one each night
  free (`adventure.free_harsh`, `museum_night.harsh`).
- **Prompted adjustments.** Loose ends ("where does the Knight of Shades turn up?") and favors ("one way it helps,
  one way it hurts").
- **An inner voice.** Euryale's interludes upstairs.

## Where it lives

See `AGENTS.md` ("This branch") for the file table and the checks. In short: the adventure is data in
`game/data/museum/`; `game/model/adventure.lua` enters it and keeps its state in `campaign.adv`;
`museum_week.lua` and `museum_night.lua` run the week and the night; `game/rules/museum.lua` holds the book's
monsters; the screens are `game/ui/museum_*.lua`.

## How it tested (October 30)

Night scores are the table's average fun out of 100.

| DM (12 seeds each) | Sessions | Endings | Build-up nights | Boss night |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Careful | 3 (one run 4) | 8 escape, 4 fallen | 77 | 60 |
| Random | 3 to 5 | 6 fallen, 4 bargain, 2 escape | 60 | 58 |
| Careless | 3 to 5 | 6 fallen, 3 bargain, 3 escape | 42 | 58 |

- The real engine plays the whole prologue (`MODES=8 TIMEOUT=2500 tools/tests/engine_check.sh out`). Its first
  run caught a build that left out the museum's night (a module reached only through `util.lazy`), now caught by
  `tools/tests/require_check.py`.
- The night scores caught two pacing holes: a session of just the stairs (scored 8) and a one-scene boss night
  (35). The stairs now fold into boss night (`adventure.boss_week`), a night never opens by going straight up
  them, the floor's last room is finished rather than stranded (`museum_night.FINISH_FLOOR`), and boss night
  ends with Flitwidget's thanks as a scene (`museum_night.thanks`).
- 178 game-night fights in the sweep, 3 lost (random and careless DMs); a lost boss fight sends the party home
  to rest. The fights may be soft; the playtest decides.
- "Her pack" (huntlings that ran) reaches the boss in almost every run; the "ally" ending never came up with bots.

## Maps and open floors (later on October 30)

The goal of this round: more agency for the DM and for the players.

- **The book's maps, in our own art.** `tools/maps/` turns an adventure's map pages into boards: `import_map.py`
  finds the grid and guesses each square, `draft_to_spec.py` crops and turns a room into a 20 x 13 spec,
  and the spec is finished by hand in the kit's pieces and our own words. `build_maps.py` checks every spec
  (on the mat, starts free, every place reachable, walls closed) and writes `game/data/map_library.lua`.
  All ten of the museum's rooms play on their own boards now; the street keeps the market.
- **A museum prop kit.** Thirty new pieces (paintings, glass cases, dioramas, the model city, scared statues,
  the lectern, Euryale's couch, marble floor) in the map maker's new Museum tab, so the DM can build with them.
- **The map repository**, in prep and on game night: this adventure's rooms, your own maps, plain boards to
  start from and more places. Set a room's map, open a side room off any room, set the next scene's map
  mid-game, or make, copy and delete your own maps.
- **Open floors.** The two closing rules are gone: the rooms join as the book's doors do, and the party can
  go back. Players only go back with a purpose (an explorer's missed corner, a DM hint, a room they walked
  past, the shop, a rest, or running from a lost fight), and at most twice a night.
- **The planning board** draws the museum as the book's floor plans: ground floor, second floor, top floor,
  with where the party is, what's done and what they walked past.

## What the friends are thinking (later still on October 30)

The feedback's "some barks could be internal dialogue": a pale cloud over a friend's panel is what they're
thinking and not saying, so the DM can read the table and answer it (`game/model/thoughts.lua`).

- **What they think about.** A want that's been nagging them (a turn, a mystery, danger, a chat, something
  shiny), their take on a call that just cost them, a long fight losing the story lovers, the week they've had
  (tired, stressed, distracted, celebrating, chatty), a roll that looked convenient or a harsh call out of
  nowhere, and Pip's player deciding to go off alone, a beat before she does. Now and then, a happy one.
- **What you can do.** Two answers each, small real table moves (give them the next moment, let them notice
  something, raise the stakes, a snack break, a quiet word, roll the next one in the open, catch her eye), or
  let it be. The wrong one for the person backfires: hand Sam the spotlight and he shrinks.
- **Empathy decides how many you catch**: 2 a night when you're Clueless, up to 5 when you Read minds. A good
  read grows Empathy, once a night. The report says how many you caught and answered.
- **The sims.** About two thoughts a night. Answering them is roughly neutral on the night scores (the
  answers are small), which is right: they're for reading the table. The sweep also found a table that went
  up the stairs after one room had a night of one room (scored 17), so nobody wants the stairs early in the
  evening while rooms are left on the floor.

## Reading them: an Insight roll, and knowing your friends (October 30, evening)

Catching a thought now only shows its tell ("Mara keeps glancing back at her notes"). To know what it is,
you read them: an Insight roll on the card, d20 + your Empathy + how well you know that friend, against how
hidden the thought is (a plan or a doubt hides better than a yawn) and how hard they are to read (Dev's face
says it all; Sam keeps things to himself; Jess is an open book, except about her plans). The card shows the
odds before you roll.

- **A clear read** hears the words. **Within 4** gets the gist (what they want, not the words). **A miss**
  can't tell: ask them straight out (two table minutes, and Sam squirms at being asked) or let it be. **A
  natural 1 misreads them**: the card shows a different want, and only at the next scene does it dawn on you
  what it really was. **A natural 20** reads them like a book.
- **How well you know each friend** (`game/model/familiar.lua`) runs from "New to you" to "Like a book": a
  night together, a clear read, an answer that lands and a talk in prep all count (reading one friend all night
  teaches only so much). It's the read's second bonus, dots on each player card in prep, and the report says
  who you know better. It's the first piece of a fuller you-as-a-DM progression.
- Reads roll on their own dice, so reading the table never changes what happens at it.
