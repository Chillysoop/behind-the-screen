# Conner's feedback on the November 4 build (part 1; more coming, don't implement yet)

Screenshots: shot_1 (the street, session 1: frowning faces, the camera), shot_2 (the fork's read-aloud saying what
each player wants), shot_3 (Mara's bubble over the read-aloud panel), shot_4 (Sam's vote line), shot_5 (the
"Something to find: the duellist" card).

1. The friends start session 1 frowning. They should start happy enough to at least be smiling.
2. When a friend's model flips to their character as they talk in character, the character's head should be above the
   table. Adjust the middle camera view so everyone and their faces fit: zoom out slightly, or make the player models
   slightly smaller, or both (shot_1).
3. After the DM sets a DC themselves, the next dialogue box shouldn't say "you set it yourself".
4. The topiary (garden briars): check the book for how they wake. Today a line says they're not bushes, they're
   creatures, before anything triggers; the players shouldn't know until something does. Check the whole encounter.
   In the latest run "the smoke rises" over the creatures although they weren't burned (unless a fire spell did it).
5. Warning about the tentacles ("are you sure?"), the DM narrates "Pip thinks better": the DM must never say what a
   character is thinking. The players give their decision to the table themselves; the DM only gives the warning or
   guidance, and only when asked.
6. "The metal statue hasn't moved, yet": never telegraph hidden or secret things. Sweep the book for DM knowledge vs
   player knowledge.
7. Pathing (forks) like shot_2 shouldn't plainly say what the players want. Show small pop-ups under the story window
   with DM intuition rolls: what the DM thinks the players would want, from reading them. As the DM and the friends
   grow familiar over the campaign, the intuition gets more accurate. Today it reads like the DM reads out what the
   players want; never tell the players what they're thinking, feeling or wanting to do.
8. As players grow more experienced at the table and more comfortable with each other, they talk in character more.
9. Move the DM dialogue up a bit so it doesn't clash with the character dialogue's position; fix player dialogue
   overlapping the in-/out-of-character tags (shot_3).
10. The chips, pizza and drinks get eaten and drunk, and disappear in chunks as the night goes on: a visual sign of the
    hours passing.
11. A session starts in a wood camp when the party is in the museum: stage it where they left off.
12. The Hall of Paintings' knight: check the book for how it works. Today it's a knight mini with a painting around
    it; it should probably start as a normal wall painting and only wake if interacted with. Confusing today.
13. "Behind the screen: something to find: the duellist" (shot_5): say whether Oswin already rolled a check. Poking
    around is an Investigation check in real D&D; it doesn't need to be a moment of its own, but it should feed these
    prompts: "Oswin (Sam) is poking around with a 16 Investigation check", to help the DM decide how to reward or
    penalize them.
