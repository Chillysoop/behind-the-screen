# Playtest round three: fresh eyes on everything

You're a playtester for "Behind the Screen", a cozy single-player game where you are the Dungeon Master for four
simulated friends (Mara, Dev, Jess and Sam, playing Thessaly the wizard, Brannoc the fighter, Pip the rogue and Oswin
the cleric). The game runs one published adventure, *The Museum of Nightmares*, as a prologue: Session Zero, why each
hero came, then the museum room by room over three to five weekly sessions, a boss night with Euryale the Gorgon, and a
journey recap. Between sessions there's a group chat, a prep board and a report.

You haven't seen the code and you shouldn't read it: judge only what a player would see or hear. You're reading
transcripts of whole prologues (every line the table hears, every card the DM is shown and what was picked) and/or
screenshots of the real game.

## What changed since the last playtest (so you know what to look at hard)
- 27 fixes from the last round: lines that claimed things that didn't happen, promises the game didn't keep, rules
  arguments about things nobody did, small moments that reacted to the wrong answer or jumped ahead of the story,
  location slips, repeated lines, broken look-around grammar, the break, Telpur's death, the recap leaving things out.
- Each hero has a real inventory: buying, finding, handing over and using things up are real.
- The museum's own paper minis for every monster and person, and Exhibit B (Pip's rat) beside Pip.
- The friends choose differently from run to run; a friend who lost the last vote pushes harder; the table notices habits.
- Letting Euryale escape halves Flitwidget's reward, and the boss board says so.
- A gentler first night: fewer calls, one of each kind, each with a tip.
- The heroes' own pronouns: Thessaly and Pip are she, Brannoc and Oswin he.
- One record of what happened; the museum's people remember the party (Telpur's prices, refusals, Flitwidget's thanks,
  the Lecturer reporting to Euryale); the friends have ideas of their own in a room; Pip can pickpocket anyone.
- The report and the journey tell each night as a chain of "so" and "but".

## What to look for
- **Untrue or contradictory:** a line that says something happened that didn't, or contradicts an earlier line; a
  "so" that isn't a real cause; someone knowing what they couldn't know; an item used that nobody has, or used twice.
- **Out of place:** the wrong room, a person who isn't there, a hero acting while they're away, a line that doesn't fit
  the moment (a joke during a death, browsing while a monster attacks).
- **Repetition:** the same line too often, the same beat every week, the same choice every run.
- **Confusing or overwhelming:** a card you can't tell how to answer, a term nobody explained, too much at once
  (especially night 1), a screen that doesn't say what it's for.
- **Flat:** stretches where nothing follows from anything ("and then, and then"), systems that never show up, people
  who should remember something and don't.
- **Broken text:** grammar, a hero called "they", a fill word left in ({char}), cut-off text, mojibake.
- **What's good:** moments that work, so we keep them.

## How to report
For each finding: a short title; severity (blocker / bad / minor / polish); which bar it fails (true, clear, alive,
fits); where (file and seed/week or screenshot name); the exact lines quoted from the transcript (short quotes), and
what would be right instead. Group duplicates and say how many times you saw each. End with the five things that
would most improve the game, and the five best moments. Don't fix anything.
