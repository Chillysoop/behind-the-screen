From "Main Campaign Design: The Open Sandbox" (https://claude.ai/artifact/UjVzYSuuqJmGo2FDs4UmSw, rev 23), section
"First, in the museum": the museum is polished with the sandbox's own systems at museum scale, so each is proven on a
finished game before a big book arrives. Conner approved these as roadmap items R7, R10, R11, R12, R13 and R14.

## The event log
The log is the single source of truth: a line never states something that isn't in it. When two systems need the same
fact, both read it from the log; neither keeps its own copy (every untrue line found in the museum reviews came from
two systems keeping their own copies). An event: who did what to whom, where, when, who saw it. People's memory is the
log read through them: someone "knows" Pip stole because an event names them as a witness.

Therefore or but, in the log: each event names the earlier event it came from, and how. "therefore": it happened
because of an earlier event (a clue was found, so the thread moves; Pip was seen, so a rumour starts). "but": it set
back or turned an earlier event (the door was open, but the Lecturer was waiting). The consequence rules write these
links themselves. In what the table hears: the session report and the journey recap tell the night as that chain,
joined with "so" and "but"; a beat with no link isn't stitched on with "and then". In the checks: the sims measure
every session the share of beats linked by therefore or but, the longest run of unlinked beats, and which rooms and
moments most often start one. Proposed museum targets: at least 4 beats in 5 linked, never more than two unlinked beats
in a row. The first measurement sets the real baseline.

## The friends' own ideas
About ten verbs: talk, ask, take, give, help, follow, go, sneak, fight, wait. Each aims at a target the world offers
(a person in the room, an item someone carries, a door, a curio). Each friend scores verb-and-target pairs from their
player type, their hero's hook, their mood tonight, unfinished business, their hero's state. The top intentions go to
the table as they do today (a friend says what they want, the others push back or join in). Most just happen, with a
small roll narrated (like small moments). Only the ones that matter come to the DM as a card: it could kill a hero, end
a thread or turn someone against the party; it would bend the book; only the DM can answer it; or it's the spotlight
moment of someone who's been out of it. The night's call budget caps them.

## The pickpocket experiment (R13's slice)
Today Pip only picks a pocket if the DM put an item on someone's sheet and marked it "pickpocket"; caught or not,
nothing follows. The slice: Pip notices something a museum person carries (nobody planted it; it's in their pockets);
Jess says so out loud, someone may object; if it matters it comes to the DM as a card (allow, roll, or no); Sleight of
Hand; a miss means someone sees it (an event with a witness); the person's opinion drops, and later they act on it (a
refusal, a higher price, a word to Flitwidget). What's taken goes into the hero's inventory (F10's new inventory).

## Consequence rules (general, the book only writes exceptions)
- A hero is seen doing harm (theft, violence, a broken promise): the victim's opinion of the party drops, less so their
  friends'. Word spreads from the witness.
- A hero helps someone: their opinion rises, and they tell the people they know.
- Someone the party wronged meets them again: they act on it (a refusal, a higher price, a trap, a chance to make amends).
Opinions: a short scale from hostile to devoted; they change prices, what people say, and whether they help or warn.
